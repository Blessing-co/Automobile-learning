FROM KEY-OFF TO RUNNING

How a modern car starts

Study the stages in order. Exact behaviour differs between petrol, diesel, push-button and key-start variants.

Safety: select P (automatic) or neutral (manual), apply the parking brake, keep clear of moving belts and fans, and follow the vehicle owner's manual. Never crank continuously for long periods.

Starting sequence

Secure the vehicle

Vehicle stationary; select P or neutral, apply parking brake and make sure the driver is correctly seated.

Ignition / power ON

Battery powers the instrument cluster and control modules. Warning lamps illuminate for a self-check and modules communicate.

Pre-start checks

Gear-position and immobiliser systems must permit starting. Some diesel variants display a glow-plug indicator; follow the exact vehicle procedure.

Cranking

Starter solenoid engages the pinion with the ring gear and rotates the crankshaft. The ECU monitors crank/cam signals and controls fuel and ignition as applicable.

Engine fires and stabilises

Combustion becomes self-sustaining and the starter disengages. Engine speed settles toward idle and oil pressure should build promptly.

Post-start observation

Check warning lamps, listen for abnormal noise, observe temperature and look for leaks. Never ignore oil-pressure or overheating warnings.

What makes it happen?

Battery

Supplies electrical power to control modules, relays and the starter circuit.

Starter motor

Turns the crankshaft so the engine can draw in air, compress the charge and begin combustion.

ECU / ECM

Uses sensor inputs to control fuel delivery and ignition on petrol engines.

Crankshaft and camshaft sensors

Provide engine position and speed information needed for correct injection and ignition timing.

Workshop note: if the engine cranks but does not start, separate the checks into engine speed/position signal, fuel supply, air, compression and ignition (petrol) rather than replacing parts by guesswork.

Diagnostic sequence

Verify the complaint and gather vehicle history.
Carry out a visual inspection before testing.
Test the battery, connections and charging system.
Use results to isolate the cause; do not guess.
Repair, clear relevant codes if required, then verify.
